## Brief (from the kit's RULES.txt, judge brief in full)

Write one line, 200 characters at most, in English: the elevator pitch a city
pigeon gives to investors for its startup. A judge model compares pitches
pairwise and the funnier wins. The judge weighs, most important first:

1. Delivers a one-line elevator pitch from a city pigeon to investors for its
   startup.
2. Is the funnier of the two, with a real joke that lands rather than a mild
   pun or a straight description.
3. Is original and surprising, with an idea or a twist the reader has not met
   before.
4. Keeps the pigeon's voice and city life in view, so the pitch could only
   come from a pigeon.
5. Fits one line in English within 200 characters, with every word earning
   its place.

"A good pun counts when it lands, and a dead one does not. Length for its own
sake does not count."

## Authoring prompt used in this session

Write six candidate one-line pitches in English, each at most 200 characters,
each a pigeon pitching a named startup to investors. The pitch must contain a
real joke with a payoff — not a straight description, not a dead pun. Use
startup jargon (TAM, churn, seed round, moat, burn rate) bent through pigeon
life (crumbs, statues, ledges, flying). Rank them against the five judge
criteria above, pick the strongest, then revise it once with a real change to
the text (not cosmetics) before finalizing.

## Final entry

> Investors, I present Coo-ber: the world's first rideshare for pigeons, by pigeons — no drivers, no surge pricing, infinite bread, and a 4.9-star rating from every statue in the city.
